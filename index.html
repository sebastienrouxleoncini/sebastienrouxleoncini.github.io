Project 01

A Functional Extraction Machine in 6 Months

ODEWA — R&D Engineering Internship, French PolynesiaSept 2025 – Feb 2026

Context & constraints

Pearl farming in French Polynesia generates large volumes of underwater waste with no existing extraction solution. The mission: design, build and validate an automated machine to extract, clean and sort this waste, under a marine environment, remote logistics, local fabrication only, and a hard 6-month deadline.

My role

Sole engineer owning the complete cycle: requirements, concept selection, CAD, structural sizing, fabrication, and in-situ testing.

CAD render of the first prototype

First prototype fabricated and assembled

Deployed for 4 days of testing in the lagoon of Takaroa

Prototype 1 in action during field tests

1 tonne in 2 days
of underwater waste extracted and cleaned during field validation

Design approach

The architecture was selected against five criteria: robustness in a marine environment, ease of local fabrication, maintainability by non-engineers, compatibility with different boat types, and shipping in a standard container. Structural models and FEA sized the key components before committing to fabrication.

From field data to prototype 2

Field data exposed gaps no simulation had predicted, and drove prototype 2 directly: separating extraction from cleaning, with a high-throughput extraction system on a barge and a dedicated cleaning module on land.

Project 02

A Sizing Tool Built from Live Plant Data

AIRARO SAS — Master Thesis, R&D Engineering InternshipMar 2026 – Sept 2026

Context & constraints

Sea Water Air Conditioning (SWAC) plants use deep cold seawater for large-scale cooling. Sizing a new plant is a high-stakes decision — expensive infrastructure that locks in decades of operating cost. AIRARO needed a way to design future systems from evidence rather than rules of thumb.

My role & approach

Six months of full-time work as my Master thesis. Starting from operating data of a live SWAC plant, I built thermal and hydraulic models validated against the real installation, then turned them into a generic sizing tool that searches the design space for the best configuration.

Technical room of a SWAC plant

COP +25%
coefficient of performance gain identified by the sizing tool

Engineering meets economics

The tool optimises while accounting for CAPEX and OPEX — the output is not just feasible, it's profitable. Integrated directly into the company's business plan.

Project 03

Compliant Feet for a Salamander Robot

BioRob Laboratory, EPFL — research projectFeb 2025 – Jul 2025

Context & constraints

Most quadruped robots use ball-joint feet and don't exploit complex ground contact. The goal: improve locomotion efficiency of an existing salamander-like robot with passive compliant feet, without modifying the rest of the platform.

Design decisions & why

I designed compliant foot structures exploring stiffness vs. energy return, prototyped them, and characterised each candidate on a dedicated test bench before integration. Bench data selected the final design.

Salamander robot with the compliant feet integrated

Robot locomotion with compliant feet

+15% locomotion efficiency
measured on the robot after integration of the compliant feet

What I learned

Bench data predicted the trend but not the exact gain — final validation always belongs on the real robot. Compliance is a tuning problem: the right stiffness depends on the task.

Project 04

Redesigning a 3-DOF Motion Platform to Carry a Human

Reconfigurable Robotics Laboratory, EPFL — research projectSept 2024 – Feb 2025

Context & constraints

An existing 3-DOF motion platform had to be upgraded to sustain a human's weight — a hard safety requirement — then driven by periodic locomotion extracted in real time from multimodal inputs.

Structural redesign & control

I reworked the structure for human safety (load cases, stiffness targets, safety factors) and implemented real-time extraction of periodic locomotion from video to drive the platform, closing the loop from raw signals to physical motion.

Motion platform with the reworked structural design

What I learned

Redesigning the platform's kinematics meant redesigning its control too — combining mechanical design, kinematics and control in the same loop, not as separate steps.

Project 07

A Simple, Effective Gripper for a Robotic Arm

Personal projectSept 2024 – Jan 2025

Design decisions & why

The gripper closes until a foam pad on each finger presses a button, so grip force is set by the mechanical design itself rather than a control loop. Finger thickness and length were tuned to get the right bend under load. A separate load sensor weighed the object while held.

Functional gripper prototype

What I learned

A well-placed sensor can replace a control loop entirely — the foam and button did in hardware what a force sensor and control logic would have done in software, at a fraction of the complexity.
